Aeroelastic divergence of a wing section

Theory · Project 10

Aeroelasticity is what happens when a structure is flexible enough that aerodynamic loads and structural deformation feed back on each other. Divergence is the static instability at the end of that feedback: a twist raises the aerodynamic moment, which raises the twist, and past a critical speed the loop runs away.

For a wing section on a torsional spring, coupling the aerodynamic lift to the structural stiffness through moment equilibrium yields a closed-form expression for the divergence dynamic pressure and the corresponding critical velocity. The project computed a baseline divergence speed of about 50 m/s for a given configuration.

What makes it a design tool is the parametric study that follows: wing area, chord, span and — crucially — the distance between the aerodynamic centre and the elastic axis all move the divergence boundary. The project showed how to redesign a section to be divergence-free up to a required 69 m/s, and extended the model to include the pitching moment, which turns the divergence condition into a singularity in the section's response.
