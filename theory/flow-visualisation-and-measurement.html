Flow visualisation and measurement

Foundations · outline to complete

A wind tunnel measures integrated forces, but understanding why a design behaves as it does needs the flow itself made visible and its local quantities measured. This chapter collects the experimental techniques, several of which appear as full projects elsewhere on the site.

To write — sources to merge:
  • Flow visualisation — smoke, tufts, surface oil-flow, the choice and suitability of tracers. MSI aerodynamics notes §4 (Applied fluid dynamics) and §6 (Track testing). McBeath, Appendix 3 (Flow visualisation fluid).
  • Pressure measurement — taps, scanners, reconstructing Cp. Cross-reference the site's NACA0012 wind-tunnel project.
  • Velocimetry — hot-wire anemometry and PIV, what each measures and its limits. Cross-reference the hot-wire and PIV projects, which already contain the method detail.
  • Track testing — how on-car aero data (pressure rakes, aero rakes, straight-line testing) differs from tunnel and CFD. MSI aerodynamics notes §6.
The three experimental projects already on the site (NACA0012 pressure, hot-wire, PIV) are the worked examples for this chapter. It should introduce each technique in general terms and then point to those projects for the applied detail, rather than duplicating them.
