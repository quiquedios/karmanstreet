Running and trusting a simulation

Foundations

A converged-looking simulation is not the same as a correct one. Two disciplines separate a result that can be reported from one that merely exists: confirming the solution has converged, and confirming it no longer depends on the mesh.

Convergence

As the solver iterates, the residuals — the degree to which the discretised equations are not yet satisfied — should fall and settle. But residuals are a necessary condition, not a sufficient one. The quantity actually being sought is an integrated force coefficient, and it is entirely possible for residuals to plateau while CL or CD is still drifting. The rule that follows is simple: monitor the coefficient itself, and treat the run as converged only when the coefficient has settled, not when the residuals look tidy.

For an unsteady or marginally stable case the coefficient will oscillate rather than flatten, and convergence then means a statistically stationary signal. The front-wing pipeline on this site handles exactly this by detecting when the oscillation stays within a ±2% band and averaging the tail — a concrete instance of the general principle.

Mesh independence

Because the answer depends on the mesh, demonstrating that it has stopped depending on the mesh is mandatory, not optional. The procedure is to refine systematically — typically three levels — and track the coefficients until successive refinements no longer change them meaningfully. Done formally, this is a grid-convergence study: the discretisation error is estimated from the differences between levels and the observed order of convergence, which is what lets you state a number with an error bar rather than a hopeful single value.

In an optimization context this matters even more than in a one-off simulation. If the discretisation error is comparable to the performance differences between candidate designs, the optimizer is chasing numerical noise rather than aerodynamics. The grid-convergence study is therefore what licenses the whole design loop, not a formality tacked on at the end.
