The source panel method for potential flow

Theory · Project 13

The source panel method is a classic way to compute inviscid, incompressible flow around a body without meshing the whole domain — you only discretise the surface.

The body's surface is broken into straight panels, each carrying an unknown source strength. Enforcing the boundary condition that no flow crosses the surface at each panel turns the problem into a linear system for those strengths. Solve it, and you can reconstruct the surface velocity and the pressure coefficient everywhere.

Because it is a boundary method it is cheap and illuminating, and it is an ideal setting to study convergence: as the panel count rises the solution approaches the exact answer, with residuals falling monotonically. The accompanying project demonstrated exactly this on a circular geometry, reaching near-zero residuals and physically consistent pressure distributions while exposing the cancellation errors that appear at very high panel counts.
