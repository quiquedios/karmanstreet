The method of images

Theory · sources: thesis

Potential flow theory models a fluid as inviscid and irrotational. Under those assumptions the velocity field derives from a potential satisfying Laplace's equation, which is linear — and linearity is what makes the whole approach useful, because complicated flows can be built by superposing simple elementary solutions.

Building a flow field

Starting from first principles with the Laplace equation and irrotational flow, the global flow field is constructed by superposing elementary solutions: a uniform stream plus a point vortex, in the case studied. Each elementary solution is trivially known; the sum of them is not, and can represent surprisingly rich behaviour.

Enforcing boundaries

Boundaries are handled with the method of images. To enforce that a solid wall lets no flow through it, mirror-image singularities are placed on the other side of the wall; their combined field automatically satisfies the no-penetration condition on the plane of symmetry. It costs nothing computationally and it is exact.

The Milne-Thomson circle theorem is the same idea specialised to a circular boundary. In the project it was used to account for the presence of a semi-cylinder near the wall, giving the full complex potential and the corresponding velocity field for the combined configuration.

Getting forces out

The problem was nondimensionalized using the key parameters, which is what allows parametric studies of flow behaviour and scalability rather than a single answer. A MATLAB implementation then computed velocity fields, identified stagnation points via numerical solvers, and generated streamline contour maps for different vortex strengths, revealing flow detachment and recirculation effects.

Finally, deriving the pressure coefficient distribution through Bernoulli's equation and integrating it over the surface gives the aerodynamic forces. That yielded an equilibrium condition at Λ = 0.397, the nondimensional vortex strength at which the net force vanishes.

Potential flow gets the pressure field and the forces qualitatively right over the front of a body and fails over the rear, where separation dominates and the inviscid assumption collapses. It is a tool for insight and for fast first estimates, not for drag prediction.
