Airfoils and finite wings

Foundations · outline to complete

The preceding chapters explain the physics of force generation on a section. This chapter turns that into the practical language of airfoils and wings: how a section is described, how its performance is read from a polar, and what changes when a wing has finite span.

To write — sources to merge:
  • Airfoil geometry and nomenclature — chord, camber, thickness, the NACA numbering system. Katz, Race Car Aerodynamics, Ch. 4 (Airfoils: Basic Definitions). Benzing, Ali/Wings, §2 (Wing Nomenclature and Characteristics).
  • Section coefficients and the polar — cl, cd, cm vs α; the drag bucket; reading peak L/D. Katz Ch. 4; McBeath, Competition Car Aerodynamics, Ch. 5. Cross-reference the site's Reading an airfoil polar note.
  • Finite wings — aspect ratio, the transition from 2D section data to 3D wing performance, lift-curve-slope reduction. Katz Ch. 4 (Finite Wings); Benzing §4 (Airfoils of Finite Length).
  • Basic lifting-surface interactions — how adjacent surfaces influence one another. Katz Ch. 4 (Basic Lifting Surface Interactions).
Much of the physics here is already covered from first principles in Part II (lift generation, induced drag). This chapter should stay descriptive and practical — the vocabulary and the design charts — and point back to those chapters for the mechanisms rather than repeat them.
