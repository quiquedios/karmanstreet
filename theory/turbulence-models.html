Turbulence models

Theory · from TFM §2.1

With the Boussinesq hypothesis established, the eddy-viscosity models are classified by how they obtain the turbulent viscosity.

The model families

Models built on the Boussinesq hypothesis are known as eddy-viscosity models, classified by the number of additional transport equations they solve to obtain µt: zero-equation (algebraic) models, one-equation models such as Spalart–Allmaras, and two-equation models, which are the most widely used in external aerodynamics.

The two classical two-equation models are k-ε and k-ω. Both solve a transport equation for k, but differ in the second variable: the former solves for the dissipation rate ε, the latter for the specific dissipation rate ω = ε/(β*k). This apparently minor difference has a decisive practical consequence. k-ε is robust and accurate in free-stream, fully turbulent regions but behaves poorly near walls and under adverse pressure gradients — precisely where boundary layers thicken and separate. k-ω resolves the near-wall region accurately without damping functions, but is overly sensitive to the free-stream value of ω far from the wall.

Why k-ω SST

The k-ω SST (Shear Stress Transport) model proposed by Menter was formulated precisely to combine the strengths of both. Through a blending function based on distance to the nearest wall, it behaves as k-ω near the wall — capturing the boundary layer and its response to pressure gradients faithfully — and gradually switches to k-ε in the free stream. In addition, the SST formulation limits the eddy viscosity in accordance with the transport of turbulent shear stress in adverse-pressure-gradient boundary layers, which significantly improves its prediction of the onset and extent of flow separation.

That last property is what makes it the natural choice for a front wing: a loaded, multi-element geometry in ground effect, where the flow is dominated by thick boundary layers developing against strong adverse pressure gradients and is perpetually on the verge of separating. Those are the very conditions under which k-ω SST is most reliable and plain k-ε and k-ω least so, which is why it has become the accepted standard for external and motorsport aerodynamics.
