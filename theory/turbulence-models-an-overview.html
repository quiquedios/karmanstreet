Turbulence models: an overview

Theory · Thesis §2.1.2

A turbulence model supplies the missing relationship between the Reynolds stresses and the mean flow. The most common engineering family uses Boussinesq's hypothesis, which assumes the Reynolds stresses behave like an extra, turbulence-generated viscosity.

The workhorse two-equation models — k-ε and k-ω — solve transport equations for turbulent kinetic energy and a second scale (dissipation or specific dissipation). k-ε is robust in the free stream but weak near walls; k-ω is the reverse. The widely used k-ω SST model blends the two, using k-ω near the wall and k-ε away from it, and is a sensible default for aerodynamic flows with adverse pressure gradients and mild separation.

No RANS model is universal; each encodes assumptions that hold well for some flows and poorly for others. Knowing which model suits the flow at hand — and validating it — is part of the craft.
