Wall treatment and y+

Theory · Thesis §2.1.2

Near a wall the turbulent boundary layer has a well-known layered structure. The dimensionless wall distance y+ measures how far the first mesh point sits from the wall in viscous units, and it decides how you must treat that region.

The law of the wall

The law of the wall says the near-wall velocity profile collapses onto a universal shape when plotted in these units: a linear viscous sublayer very close to the wall, a logarithmic region further out, and a buffer layer between. Turbulence models rely on this structure.

Two strategies

Either you resolve the sublayer — placing the first cell at y+ of order 1 and meshing right down to the wall — or you use a wall function that models the near-wall region and expects the first cell in the log layer (y+ roughly 30–300). Landing y+ in the buffer layer, between the two, is the worst of both worlds. Getting y+ right is one of the most consequential meshing decisions in a CFD study.
