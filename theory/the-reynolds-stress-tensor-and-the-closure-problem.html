The Reynolds stress tensor and the closure problem

Theory · from TFM §2.1

The RANS equations introduce a new unknown — the Reynolds stress tensor. This is the closure problem, and the reason a turbulence model is needed.

The Reynolds stress tensor and the closure problem

That term is the Reynolds stress tensor, a symmetric second-order tensor representing the mean momentum transport caused by turbulent fluctuations. Its three diagonal components are normal stresses and its six off-diagonal components shear stresses; symmetry reduces the nine components to six independent unknowns.

Physically, it accounts for the fact that turbulent eddies continuously exchange momentum between adjacent layers of fluid — an effect entirely absent from the mean-field description, but one that dominates the behaviour of the flow.

The appearance of these six new unknowns without any additional equations to determine them is the closure problem of turbulence. The averaging procedure has introduced more unknowns than equations, so the system cannot be solved as it stands. Bridging that gap is precisely the role of a turbulence model, which supplies the missing relations between the Reynolds stresses and the mean flow — at the cost of introducing empirical values.
