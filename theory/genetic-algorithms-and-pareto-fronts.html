Genetic algorithms and Pareto fronts

Theory · Thesis §2.1.4

Real designs rarely have a single objective. An F1 front wing wants maximum downforce and minimum drag, and those pull in opposite directions. Multi-objective optimization is about mapping the trade-off rather than pretending there is one best answer.

Genetic algorithms

A genetic algorithm borrows from evolution: it maintains a population of candidate designs, evaluates their fitness, and breeds the next generation by combining and mutating the best. It needs no gradients and copes well with rugged, high-dimensional design spaces — which is why it pairs naturally with a cheap surrogate that it can call thousands of times.

The Pareto front

The output is not one design but a Pareto front: the set of designs for which you cannot improve one objective without sacrificing another. Every point on it is an optimal compromise; choosing among them is an engineering decision. In the thesis, NSGA-II produces the front, and specific points are then picked per circuit using how much of each lap is spent with DRS open.
