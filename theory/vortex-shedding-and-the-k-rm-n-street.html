Vortex shedding and the Kármán street

Theory · Project 28

Flow past a bluff body such as a circular cylinder is one of the richest problems in fluid dynamics — and the origin of this site's name. What happens depends strongly on the Reynolds number.

At very low Reynolds number the flow is steady and symmetric. As it rises the wake lengthens, then becomes unstable, and past a critical value the cylinder begins to shed vortices alternately from each side. These roll downstream in the regular, staggered pattern known as the von Kármán vortex street.

The shedding is periodic, and its frequency is captured by the dimensionless Strouhal number, which settles to roughly 0.2 over a wide range of Reynolds numbers. The project traced this whole progression numerically from Re ≈ 1 to 800, watching the separation angle move forward, the wake go unsteady, and the shedding frequency climb — from about 2 Hz at Re = 100 to 25 Hz at Re = 800 — while the Strouhal number stayed in its narrow band.
