Laminar vs turbulent boundary layers

Theory · Thesis §2.1.1

Right against any surface is a thin region where viscosity dominates and the velocity climbs from zero at the wall to the freestream value: the boundary layer. It comes in two regimes.

A laminar boundary layer is smooth and orderly, with low skin friction but little resistance to adverse pressure gradients — it separates easily. A turbulent boundary layer is chaotic and thicker, with higher skin friction but far better mixing, so it stays attached longer. The trade is fundamental: turbulence costs friction drag but buys separation resistance.

The switch between them, transition, is governed largely by Reynolds number and surface disturbances. A great deal of practical aerodynamics is really about managing where transition and separation happen — sometimes even tripping the flow turbulent deliberately to keep it attached.
