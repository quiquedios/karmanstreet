Bluff-body drag and flow separation

Theory · Project 30

A streamlined body carries the flow gently to its trailing edge; a bluff body cannot, and the flow separates, leaving a wide low-pressure wake. That wake is where most of a bluff body's drag comes from.

On a circular cylinder, ideal inviscid theory predicts the flow stays attached all the way to the rear (separation at 90° from the front in the potential-flow picture). Reality is different: viscous effects cause the boundary layer to separate earlier — around 64° in the experimental case studied — and the resulting wake sets a pressure-drag coefficient far above the inviscid value.

By reconstructing the pressure coefficient around the surface and integrating it, the project computed drag coefficients in the range 1.2–2.9 and showed the expected quadratic growth of drag force with speed, tying the measured separation point directly to the drag penalty and validating against the classical experiments of Norberg.
