Bayesian optimization and active learning

Theory · from TFM §2.1

With a probabilistic surrogate in hand, Bayesian optimization uses its uncertainty to choose which design to evaluate next.

Bayesian optimization

With a probabilistic surrogate available, Bayesian optimization is the strategy used to find the optimum of the expensive function with as few evaluations as possible. Rather than optimizing the surrogate once, it proceeds iteratively: at each step an acquisition function, built from the GP's mean and variance, identifies the most informative design to evaluate next, balancing exploitation (sampling where the mean predicts a good value) against exploration (sampling where the variance is high and the model is uncertain).

That design is evaluated with the true CFD, the GP is re-fitted with the new data, and the cycle repeats. This is the active learning that progressively sharpens the surrogate precisely where it matters, and it is what makes a plain space-filling LHS seed sufficient as a starting point.

For a single objective, a standard choice is Expected Improvement, measuring how much the objective is expected to exceed the best value observed so far. Because the predictive distribution is Gaussian, the expectation has a closed form depending on both mean and standard deviation: a design scores highly either when its mean is promising or when its uncertainty is large enough that a good outcome remains plausible, so the single expression balances the two automatically.
