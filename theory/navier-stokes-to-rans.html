Navier–Stokes to RANS

Theory · Thesis §2.1.2

The Navier–Stokes equations express conservation of mass and momentum for a viscous fluid. In principle they describe every eddy in a turbulent flow; in practice resolving all of them (direct numerical simulation) is far too expensive for engineering geometries.

Reynolds averaging

The engineering workaround is to split every quantity into a mean and a fluctuation, then average the equations. This gives the Reynolds-Averaged Navier–Stokes (RANS) equations, which solve only for the mean flow — vastly cheaper.

The closure problem

Averaging the nonlinear convection term produces new unknowns, the Reynolds stresses, representing the momentum transport by turbulent fluctuations. There are now more unknowns than equations: the closure problem. Turbulence models exist precisely to close this gap by relating the Reynolds stresses back to the mean flow.
