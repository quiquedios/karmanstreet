The boundary layer

Foundations

Right against any surface the no-slip condition forces the fluid velocity to zero. It climbs from there to the freestream value across a thin region where viscosity cannot be ignored no matter how large the Reynolds number is: the boundary layer. Almost everything that makes real aerodynamics deviate from ideal theory happens inside it.

Growth and the two regimes

The layer begins thin at the leading edge and thickens downstream as more fluid is slowed by the wall. It starts laminar — orderly, low-friction, but fragile — and after some distance transitions to turbulent: thicker, higher-friction, but far better at mixing high-momentum fluid down towards the wall. The transition point depends on the Reynolds number and on surface disturbances, and it can be forced deliberately, a fact Part IV relies on when tripping a wind-tunnel model.

Why attachment is a balance

Whether the layer stays attached is decided by the pressure gradient it runs into. Near a solid surface the fluid has already lost momentum to viscosity, so it has little to spare. A favorable gradient (pressure falling downstream) accelerates it and keeps it attached; an adverse gradient (pressure rising) drains what remains, and if it is strong enough the near-wall flow stalls and then reverses — separation. A turbulent layer resists this longer than a laminar one precisely because it continually replenishes the near-wall momentum by mixing.

Attachment, then, is not a property of the surface but the outcome of a contest between the imposed pressure gradient and the momentum left in the boundary layer. This single idea explains stall, explains bluff-body drag, and sets the ceiling on how hard any wing can be loaded — themes that recur through Parts II and V.

Separation is where the cheap models give up. Potential flow ignores the boundary layer entirely and so cannot predict it; the boundary-layer equations themselves break down at the separation point. Capturing it reliably is a large part of why the applied work in these notes leans on RANS CFD with a turbulence model chosen for exactly this behaviour.
