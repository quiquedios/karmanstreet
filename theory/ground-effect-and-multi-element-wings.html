Ground effect and multi-element wings

Theory · Thesis §2.1.1

Two features dominate high-downforce aerodynamics: proximity to the ground, and the use of several elements working together.

Ground effect

Bring a wing (or a shaped underbody) close to the ground and the gap behaves like a converging-diverging channel. The flow accelerates, pressure drops, and downforce rises sharply as clearance shrinks — up to the point where the boundary layers choke the gap and it collapses. The strong dependence of downforce on ride height is why ground-effect cars are so sensitive to their platform.

Multi-element wings

A single element can only be pushed so hard before it stalls. Splitting it into a cascade — a main plane plus one or more flaps, each in its own slot — lets each element run at a healthy loading while the slots re-energise the boundary layer and delay separation. This is how F1 front wings reach the very high lift coefficients they do, and it is the geometry family the thesis parametrises and optimises.
