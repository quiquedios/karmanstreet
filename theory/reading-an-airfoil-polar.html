Reading an airfoil polar

Theory · Project 12

An airfoil polar is the compact summary of how a section performs. In its most useful form it plots the lift coefficient against the drag coefficient (or against angle of attack) across the operating range, letting you read off performance at a glance.

The key quantities are the lift-to-drag ratio (aerodynamic efficiency, whose peak marks the most economical operating point), the angle of attack at which that peak occurs, and the derived axial and normal force coefficients and centre of pressure location. Together they tell you not just how much lift a section makes but at what cost and with what pitching behaviour.

This is the basis of profile selection. In the glider-aerodynamics project, comparing polars at a Reynolds number of 10⁶ is what singled out the NACA 4412 section, whose peak L/D of about 129 at just over 5° angle of attack made it the efficient choice for range.
