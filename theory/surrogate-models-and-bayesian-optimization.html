Surrogate models and Bayesian optimization

Theory · Thesis §2.1.4

If every design evaluation costs a CFD run, optimising directly on the simulation is prohibitive. A surrogate model sidesteps this: it is a cheap-to-evaluate function fitted to the designs you have already computed, standing in for the expensive simulation during the search.

Gaussian processes

A common surrogate is the Gaussian process, which predicts not just a value at an unseen design but an uncertainty on that prediction. That uncertainty is what makes intelligent search possible.

Bayesian optimization and active learning

Bayesian optimization uses the surrogate's prediction and its uncertainty together, via an acquisition function, to decide which design to evaluate next — balancing exploitation of promising regions against exploration of uncertain ones. Each new CFD result is fed back to sharpen the surrogate (active learning) until it is trustworthy enough to freeze and optimise on. The thesis fits its Gaussian process to 70×2 designs — about 2.7 samples per dimension — before refining it this way.
