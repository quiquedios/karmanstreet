Motorsport air-brake design and load analysis

Project · Project 35

End-to-end design and engineering definition of a deployable aerodynamic air-brake for a high-performance vehicle, developed in CATIA V5 through iterative design to maximise drag generation and stability under high-speed braking.

I defined the worst-case load scenario — full deployment at 490 km/h — computing a dynamic pressure of 11.3 kPa and 4.9 kN of resulting force to size the structure and check support stresses above 800 kPa, including fatigue from vortex-induced vibration. The design was translated into a manufacturable carbon-epoxy prepreg component (200 g/m²) with internal spars and ribs to carry bending and torsion, meeting IT8 fits and an N4 surface finish.

Beyond the part itself I contributed to tooling and mould design, production planning for 30 prototypes plus 400 units, and a supplier-ready Request for Quotation package with drawings, bill of materials, tolerances and cost estimate.
