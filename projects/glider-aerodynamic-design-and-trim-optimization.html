Glider aerodynamic design and trim optimization

Project · Project 29

Design and optimisation of a high-efficiency glider under mass and geometric constraints, maximising aerodynamic performance while holding longitudinal stability and trim. The final configuration used a high-aspect-ratio wing (AR = 30, span 3.0 m, mass 750 g) on an Eppler 186 section, with a NACA 0010 tail to minimise drag.

Using viscous and inviscid polars I located the trimmed condition where peak efficiency (L/D = 32 in viscous flow) coincides with static stability (Cmα < 0, Cm > 0), achieved by tuning the centre-of-gravity position to 200–215 mm — directly linking mass distribution to stability and performance.

The design reached a glide range of about 640 m and around 32 s of endurance from a 20 m launch, at a glide angle of −1.79°. A sensitivity study over aspect ratio, twist, tail size and tail moment arm mapped the trade-offs between stability, efficiency and performance.
