Glider aerodynamic design and trim optimization

Project · Project 29

Design and optimization of a high-efficiency glider configuration under mass and geometric constraints, focused on maximizing aerodynamic performance while ensuring longitudinal stability and trim conditions.

Configuration

The final configuration featured a high aspect ratio wing (AR = 30, span = 3.0 m, mass = 750 g) using an Eppler 186 airfoil for maximum aerodynamic efficiency, combined with a NACA 0010 tail configuration to minimize the drag contribution.

Method

Aerodynamic analysis was performed using both viscous and inviscid polars, identifying the trimmed flight conditions where maximum efficiency coincides with stable behaviour (Cmα < 0, Cm > 0). This was achieved by optimizing the centre-of-gravity position to x = 200–215 mm, directly linking mass distribution to aerodynamic stability and performance.

Results

Peak efficiency reached CL/CD = 32 under viscous conditions. The final design achieved a glide range of about 640 m and endurance of around 32 s from a 20 m altitude, at a glide angle of γ = −1.79°.

A parametric sensitivity study was additionally conducted, evaluating the impact of aspect ratio, geometric twist, tail size and tail moment arm on trim angle, range and endurance, identifying the key trade-offs between stability, efficiency and performance.
