Finite-volume Euler solver from first principles

Project · Project 49

A finite-volume CFD solver written from scratch to simulate compressible flow in a 2-D channel with a geometric perturbation, solving the Euler equations with a cell-centred formulation.

The solver used central differencing for the fluxes with artificial dissipation, and a four-stage Runge–Kutta time integration with adaptive, CFL-based time stepping. I implemented full boundary-condition handling for subsonic inlet and outlet, ensuring the correct characteristic behaviour, and validated against Hirsch's reference data — matching the pressure-coefficient distribution and confirming mesh independence at a 75×25 grid.

Parametric studies over freestream Mach number (0.3–0.7) captured the onset of compressibility effects and shock formation at the higher end, alongside analyses of entropy variation, outlet-pressure sensitivity and convergence via residual reduction and mass-flow balance.
