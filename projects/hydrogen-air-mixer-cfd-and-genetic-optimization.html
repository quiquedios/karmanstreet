Hydrogen–air mixer: CFD and genetic optimization

Project · Project 34

Design and optimisation of a passive hydrogen–air mixing system to improve combustion stability through better outlet-mixture uniformity, exploiting Venturi-induced acceleration, controlled turbulence and hydrogen's high diffusivity. The device combined a Venturi flume with radial hydrogen injectors and a downstream cylinder array to deliberately trigger vortex shedding and enhance turbulent mixing.

The CFD was high-fidelity: unsteady RANS in Ansys Fluent with k-ω SST turbulence and species transport, capturing the coupled diffusion–turbulence physics, validated against canonical benchmarks for Strouhal number and drag. I then automated the entire workflow with Python (PyFluent), converting GUI-driven simulations into scriptable pipelines for parametric study without manual intervention.

That automated framework was coupled to a genetic algorithm (PyGAD) that iteratively optimised the inlet conditions to maximise a custom outlet-uniformity index, delivering a clear improvement over the nominal design while keeping the architecture mechanically simple, with no moving parts.
